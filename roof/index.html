Apostolic witnessJesus and the promise to David

After the resurrection, Jesus tells His disciples that repentance and forgiveness will be preached in His name. In Acts 13, Paul announces Jesus as the Savior God brought from David’s line. His sermon moves from David to Jesus’ resurrection and to the offer of forgiveness. Read the end of the sermon and notice what Paul asks his listeners to trust.

Clement of Rome · Late first centuryWhat a church can learn from David

Writing from Rome to a church divided by conflict, Clement calls David an example of humility. He sets God’s praise of David beside David’s own prayer for mercy, then quotes Psalm 51 at length. In the next chapter, he asks the Corinthians to seek peace. In Clement’s hands, David’s prayer teaches a whole community how to turn back.

John Chrysostom · Fourth centuryHow repentance changes conduct

Chrysostom looks beyond David’s fasting to a later scene. When Shimei curses David and one of David’s men offers to kill him, David stops him. Chrysostom sees the king’s refusal to retaliate as evidence of a humbled heart. Where would a change of heart show up in the way you use the power you have?

Augustine of Hippo · Late antiquityWhat to imitate in David
“For many men will to fall with David, and will not to rise with David.”

Augustine warns against using David’s story as permission to fall. He hears the psalm speak to two people at once: the person tempted to repeat David’s sin, and the person who has already fallen and needs to know that they can still get up. Ask which of those two needs to hear the psalm today.
