Medicine
of Immortality

The one who hides. The gift recognized. The closed hand opened.

Listen on FIND ME

I

The door I locked

The song begins on the inside of a locked room. Someone heard Him coming and kept the door shut. The opening lines echo the oldest hiding place in Scripture:

And the LORD God called unto Adam, and said unto him, Where art thou? And he said, I heard thy voice in the garden, and I was afraid, because I was naked; and I hid myself.

Genesis 3:9–10 · KJV

The song alludes to Genesis 3. It doesn’t retell it.

II

Strange and ancient weight

The title comes from Ignatius, bishop of Antioch, who wrote letters to churches while he was being taken under guard to Rome, where he expected to die. The song gathers more than one of his letters, and one later teacher. Each reading below is commentary, not Scripture.

Ignatius of Antioch · Letter to the Ephesians, chapter 20

“…breaking one and the same bread, which is the medicine of immortality…”

The phrase belongs to a picture of a gathered church. Ignatius hopes to hear that the Ephesians meet together in one faith in Jesus Christ, “of the seed of David according to the flesh”, that they obey their bishop and presbytery “with an undivided mind”, and that they break one bread. He calls that bread the medicine of immortality, an antidote against dying, so that they live for ever in Jesus Christ. It is early Christian teaching about the Eucharist, not a promise of physical cure.

Read Ephesians, chapter 20

Ignatius of Antioch · Letter to the Romans, chapter 7

“I have no delight in corruptible food, nor in the pleasures of this life. I desire the bread of God, the heavenly bread, the bread of life, which is the flesh of Jesus Christ…”

A different letter and a different moment. Here Ignatius writes to the church in Rome about his own coming death. What he longs for is Christ Himself, and he goes on to name “His blood, which is incorruptible love and eternal life.” The song’s first verse borrows this longing.

Read Romans, chapter 7

Irenaeus of Lyons · Against Heresies, Book IV, 18.5

“…the bread, which is produced from the earth, when it receives the invocation of God, is no longer common bread, but the Eucharist, consisting of two realities, earthly and heavenly…”

This is Irenaeus, not Ignatius, writing later in the second century. He argues that the Eucharist shows God’s care for the body: our bodies, receiving it, are “no longer corruptible, having the hope of the resurrection.” The song’s second verse (“Bread from the earth, no longer common bread / Earthly and heavenly”) draws on this passage.

Read Against Heresies IV.18

Ignatius of Antioch · Letter to the Smyrnaeans, chapters 6–7

“…they confess not the Eucharist to be the flesh of our Saviour Jesus Christ, which suffered for our sins…”

Ignatius warns about teachers who said Christ only seemed to suffer. In the same breath he notes that they have “no care for the widow, or the orphan, or the oppressed.” For him, believing that Christ truly suffered and caring for the vulnerable belong together. The song’s line “The body that suffered is the body I need most” stands near this.

Read Smyrnaeans, chapters 6–8

The Scripture underneath

I am the bread of life: he that cometh to me shall never hunger; and he that believeth on me shall never thirst.

John 6:35 · KJV

I am the living bread which came down from heaven

John 6:51 · KJV

Jesus says this to a crowd that had just been fed, in the long Bread of Life discourse (John 6:35–58).

Paul writes to Corinth because their shared meal had become a place of division. Some ate first while others went hungry, and the poor were shamed:

For in eating every one taketh before other his own supper: and one is hungry, and another is drunken.

1 Corinthians 11:21 · KJV

But let a man examine himself, and so let him eat of that bread, and drink of that cup.

1 Corinthians 11:28 · KJV

Wherefore, my brethren, when ye come together to eat, tarry one for another.

1 Corinthians 11:33 · KJV

Paul’s call to self-examination sits inside his call to wait for one another. The examination is not only private.

Read the whole chapters: John 6 (NABRE) 1 Corinthians 11 (NABRE)

III

The hand that closed

What keeps your hand closed?

There’s no right answer, and nothing is saved or sent.

What I have done

What man of you, having an hundred sheep, if he lose one of them, doth not leave the ninety and nine in the wilderness, and go after that which is lost, until he find it?

Luke 15:4 · KJV

In the parable, the shepherd does the walking. The one who is lost is carried home.

Knowing the words without living them

…and him that cometh to me I will in no wise cast out.

John 6:37 · KJV

Knowing about the gift is not the same as receiving the Giver. Jesus doesn’t ask you to arrive with the right words.

Being seen as I am

And the LORD God called unto Adam, and said unto him, Where art thou?

Genesis 3:9 · KJV

God’s first word to someone hiding is a question that calls them out, not a sentence passed on them.

Someone I won’t wait for, or won’t forgive

Wherefore, my brethren, when ye come together to eat, tarry one for another.

1 Corinthians 11:33 · KJV

Jesus tells anyone bringing a gift to the altar who remembers a brother with something against him to leave the gift, go and be reconciled first, and then come back (Matthew 5:23–24). Irenaeus reads those words alongside the Church’s offering in Against Heresies IV.18.1. The table is not received alone.

Matthew 5 (NABRE)

The song’s turn is not a hand that finally forces itself open. It is someone who comes in. “He opens from the inside.”

If you want to pray

Lord Jesus Christ, You came looking for me while I was hiding. I know the words; teach me to receive You. Open what I keep closed. Make me willing to wait for others at Your table, and bring me home to Your people. Amen.

A next step

If something stands between you and someone else, start with reconciliation, as Jesus said. If you are thinking about the Lord’s Supper, talk with your priest or pastor about how to prepare in your church. Churches differ on this, and this page doesn’t decide it for you.
